## 2026-08-02 — Instrument the site with cookieless analytics and add an inline feedback and testimonial route

- **What** — The web edition now measures its own use and asks readers what they think, neither of which it did before. Three reader-visible additions: (1) a **thumbs up / thumbs down** control at the foot of every chapter, where a thumbs-down opens an optional text box whose contents are posted to a third-party form processor and forwarded to the maintainer; (2) an inline **testimonial ask**, shown only after a thumbs-up and only to a reader who has cleared an engagement bar, linking to a new **Share Your Experience** page and a hosted consent form; (3) a new **Privacy** page, linked from the site footer. The "Send email" row in the reading-preferences panel predates this work and is retained, now doubling as the fallback offered when a thumbs-down note fails to send. Behind all of it, [Simple Analytics](https://www.simpleanalytics.com/) records page views and fourteen named events — `Notes downloaded`, `Commentary revealed`, `Funnel run`, `Cooperation table used`, `Dyslexia font toggled`, `Feedback email opened`, `Chapter rated`, `Feedback prompt shown` / `clicked` / `dismissed`, `Feedback detail sent` / `failed`, `Share experience CTA clicked`, `Feedback submitted`. No cookies are set and no consent banner is shown.
- **Where** — New reader-facing pages `privacy.qmd` and `share-your-experience.qmd`, both added to the book's back matter in `_quarto-en.yml`; a Privacy link added to the footer in `_quarto.yml`, along with the Simple Analytics script tag and the Content-Security-Policy widening its host and `api.web3forms.com` require. New scripts `assets/scripts/telemetry.js` (the only file holding vendor-specific analytics calls), `engagement.js` (the on-device ledger and the `shouldPrompt()` predicate), `feedback-prompt.js`, `chapter-rating.js`, `feedback-transport.js` (the only file holding the form processor's endpoint and key), `share-your-experience.js`, and `storage.js` (the guarded `safeGet`/`safeSet` helpers extracted out of `reading-prefs.js` so the ledger could share them). Instrumentation call sites added to `functions.js`, `funnel-experiment.js`, `cooperation-tables.js` and `reading-prefs.js`. New `.chapter-rating*` and `.feedback-prompt*` blocks in `assets/styles/main.css`. New unit tests under `tests/`, and both new pages added to `.pa11yci.json`. The About page's "Contributing and reporting issues" section became "Feedback, contributions, and reporting issues" and now signposts three routes rather than one.
- **Source reference** — n/a. Neave's PDFs have no equivalent surface: a printed course cannot ask its reader anything, and the original's only feedback route is the NZOQ's own contact details. Nothing in his text is altered, moved, or omitted by this change. It is logged here because it materially changes what a reader can do on a chapter page, and the log is where the site's reader-facing decisions and their reasoning are kept.
- **Why** — The site had been promoted to a live cohort and was getting warm verbal feedback, none of which was reaching anywhere it could be acted on or, with consent, published. It was also entirely un-instrumented: there was no evidence about which days readers actually reach, whether the 49 "Download Your Notes" buttons are ever pressed, whether the two expensive bespoke widgets (the Day 3 funnel simulation, the Day 8 cooperation tables) get used, or whether the OpenDyslexic toggle — the accessibility feature that motivated the whole project — is used at all. The stance taken was that this course spends a full day arguing against managing by numbers you do not understand, so the instrumentation is deliberately narrow: locate friction and check whether the accessibility investment is used, rather than build a dashboard that then invites tampering. Hence a curated event set small enough to reason about by hand, no scroll-depth or keystroke tracking, and a vendor chosen for being cookieless and EU-hosted rather than for feature breadth.
- **Implementation note — asking is engagement-gated and self-limiting** — The testimonial ask is computed entirely on-device from a `td:engagement` ledger in `localStorage`: it requires 30 minutes of active reading time, visits on two distinct days, and at least one content interaction, and it counts active time only while the tab is visible *and* there has been input in the last minute, so an idle foreground tab does not qualify anyone. A reader who dismisses it does not see it again for 45 days; a second dismissal silences it permanently, as does submitting the form. The per-chapter thumbs, by contrast, are shown to everyone from the first visit — they cost one click, are never repeated for a chapter already rated, and carry no follow-up unless the reader opens one.
- **Implementation note — one place where reader-written text leaves the device** — The origin is a static docroot deployed by `rsync --delete`; there is no server behind it that could receive a POST. The thumbs-down text box therefore posts client-side to [Web3Forms](https://web3forms.com/), which forwards it to the maintainer's inbox. The payload is deliberately confined to the reader's words, the chapter, and the rating — no identifier, no email address, nothing from the engagement ledger — and a unit test asserts the payload's entire key set so that adding a field has to be a deliberate act. This is the single respect in which the epic's original "nothing leaves the device beyond what a cookieless analytics vendor sees" intent was knowingly relaxed; `privacy.qmd` states plainly that the request necessarily carries the reader's IP address and user-agent to that processor, which is the one place on the site where something sent to the maintainer could in principle be traced back to a person.
- **Implementation note — what stays local** — Theme choice, the dyslexia-font toggle, Day 3 funnel-experiment progress, per-chapter ratings, and the engagement ledger all live in `localStorage` and are never transmitted. Clearing site data or using a private window resets all of it, at the cost of being asked for a testimonial again once the bar is re-cleared.
